A life kept in papers, rooms, and questions

The Arthur Valerius Sterling Foundation preserves the intellectual and household legacy of Arthur Valerius Sterling and Elizabeth Crawford Sterling: his unfinished scientific question, her history of European ideas, and the materials that remained between them.

Arthur was a physicist whose work moved toward biophysics and the problem of whether biological systems can preserve information about the past. Elizabeth was a historian with a professional life of her own. They met in Cambridge in 1948, married in 1951, and carried a shared world of books, correspondence, photographs, notebooks, and working objects across decades.

This archive is incomplete. In 2008, their daughter Catherine “Kate” Sterling began this measured public record from family papers and materials available after Arthur’s disappearance and Elizabeth’s death. Some materials are described; others survive only as references, memories, or open questions. The site was intended to grow gradually, leaving gaps visible rather than making them convenient.

Reading the record

The catalogue places documents beside the lives that gave them meaning. Arthur’s papers remain hypotheses, and the record of his disappearance remains open. Begin with the 2008 preservation project or Kate’s account of the site; the Editorial Framework gives the full reading conventions.
